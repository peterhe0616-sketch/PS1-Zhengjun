\textbf{Artifact.} Trace-or-Fake Market Game, a $3\times2$ provenance-rule simulator. Space: \url{https://huggingface.co/spaces/Peterhe123/Trust}. Files: \texttt{index.html}, \texttt{README.md}, \texttt{verify\_game.mjs}. Author: Zhengjun He. v1.0.0, 6 September 2026.

\textbf{What it does.} A developer publishing a tool answers two questions. Q1, trace file: provide one (cost $+5\%$, benefit $+20\%$), none (no change), or fake one (cost $-20\%$, benefit $-20\%$). Q2, verifier: enrol (cost $+5\%$, stability $+20\%$) or not. All indices start at $100$; the page prints a report after three runs. Nothing is stored and no network request is made.

\textbf{Payoff law and checks.} One block is read by both the game and the notebook, so they cannot disagree. Five checks pass: with no rules the fake wins; with $p=1,F=100$ honesty is unique; the status quo nets $100$; enrolment is weakly dominant; there are six cells. The law carries two mechanisms: a fine $pF$ charged only to a detected fake, and a premium $\Delta$ paid only for a verifiable trace file. Enrolment in the verifier costs $5$ and adds $20$ stability, so it is weakly dominant and the effective choice is among the three actions.

\textbf{Correction, 9 September 2026.} The first version summed a cost, a benefit and a stability index. Cost and benefit have opposite signs, so summing them let honesty win on both ($105$ vs $80$ cost, $120$ vs $80$ benefit) before any rule applied, and the sweep returned $p^{*}=0$: faking was never attractive, so the question could not be posed. The corrected law enters cost negatively and gives a fake the same market benefit as an honest deliverable. It now yields a real threshold: the fake wins with no rules ($160$ vs $135$), and at $F=50$ honesty requires $pF>25$, so $p^{*}=0.5000$; the required $p$ falls as $F$ rises ($F=100\Rightarrow0.25$; $F=400\Rightarrow0.0625$), and below $F=25$ no $p\le1$ suffices.

\textbf{Second correction, same date: the missing premium.} With the law above, honest and faked traces were paid identically, so honesty was a tax rather than an asset and was never chosen for being worth more. A market premium $\Delta$ paid only for a verifiable trace file repairs this. The two mechanisms substitute exactly: honesty requires $\Delta+pF>25$. At $\Delta=0$ the fine must do all the work ($p^{*}=0.5$ at $F=50$); at $\Delta=15$ the required rate halves to $0.2$; at $\Delta=25$ no enforcement is needed at all and honesty wins with $p=F=0$. Values are from the verified notebook run; the deployed Space should be updated to this law.

\section{Cumulative Intellectual Development}\label{app:develop}
This proposal develops my Intellectual Statement II through the \emph{2056 Nobel and Turing Laureate Program Launch Workshop}, September 7, 2026, Duke Kunshan University, IB 2050. Program Chair: Prof. Luyao Zhang. Program Discussant: Prof. Ken Rogerson. My session: Workshop 6. My role: [assigned role]. My teammates: Longfei Jing and Yongzhuo Wu.

\textbf{First change: the object of the question.} My earlier claim was that I would build mechanisms letting agents and their designers cooperate under near-total unobservability. The September 4 field evidence forced something narrower. At the Tencent Shanghai Office, plugin quality is hard to observe before adoption, so buyers rely on cheap signals. At the Shanghai Science and Technology Museum, the deep-residual-learning panel asks a general audience to trust institutional authority rather than checkable evidence. The marketplace is a market without institutional trust; the museum is institutional trust without a market. Workshop feedback sharpened the boundary: build-layer provenance already exists at near-zero cost, so the contribution must sit at the claim layer. That moved the proposal from a disclosure design to a rule design.

\textbf{Second change: the payoff-law correction, 9 September 2026.} \emph{Earlier claim:} the deployed game showed honest-plus-verified was the unique optimum, offered as evidence the rule works. \emph{Source:} the notebook run during computational practice. \emph{Revision:} the sweep returned $p^{*}=0$, showing the game summed cost with the wrong sign and penalised faking on benefit too, so honesty won regardless of any rule. The law was re-derived. \emph{Consequence:} this is substantive, not cosmetic. The original game could not pose its own question, since faking was never tempting and there was nothing for a fine to deter. The corrected law gives a real threshold ($pF>25$, so $p^{*}=0.5$ at $F=50$), which is the quantity the proposal needs. Affected: Section~3, the Section~5 table, the Open Science Statement, and rows~4--5 of Appendix~\ref{app:abstract}. A baseline that cannot fail its own test is not evidence.

\textbf{Third change: honesty was modelled as a cost, not an asset, 9 September 2026.} \emph{Earlier claim:} the corrected law showed honesty could be an equilibrium, and I treated the analysis as complete. \emph{Source:} reading the certification-design result of M{\"a}kimattila et al.~\cite{makimattila2025}, in which a sender's \emph{choice} among offered tests is itself informative, together with Spence's single-crossing condition~\cite{spence1973}. \emph{Revision:} in my law honest and faked traces were paid the same base benefit, so honesty won only by avoiding a penalty. I added a market premium $\Delta$ paid only for a verifiable trace file, making the condition $\Delta+pF>25$. \emph{Consequence:} the proposal now answers its own question. Before, it could show only that cheating does not pay; now it shows honesty can win because it is worth more, and that a premium of $25$ removes the need to fine at all. Affected: Section~2 (hypothesis, model, Table~1), Q1, the Section~5 roadmap and the 2056 statement. \emph{What remains unresolved:} $\Delta$ is still a parameter rather than a belief. If fakers can publish traces as cheaply as honest producers the signal pools and $\Delta\to0$, so making $\Delta$ endogenous is the next study.

\textbf{Two open-source tools.} (i) \emph{SLSA}~\cite{slsa}, \url{https://slsa.dev/}, v1.2, accessed 6 September 2026; repository Apache-2.0. Capability: generates non-forgeable build provenance---which commit built which artifact---so a verifier can check it. Limitation: it certifies the build chain, not whether the artifact does what its description claims. Consequence: SLSA supplies the build layer at near-zero cost, so my contribution is the claim layer it ignores. (ii) \emph{CKAN}~\cite{ckan}, \url{https://ckan.org/}, 2.11, accessed 6 September 2026; GNU AGPL-3.0. Capability: an open-data catalog with standardised metadata and public APIs. Limitation: it stores whatever publishers upload and never checks whether a dataset matches its description. Consequence: CKAN is the natural substrate for the trace file, since a claim would deep-link to an exact dataset that the verifier recomputes.
